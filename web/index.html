How it works

Four steps, from forecast to crew.

Everything runs ahead of the storm, so the fix lands while it is still cheap.

01

Watch the sky

Pull the rain forecast for Sofia. A heavy-rain window arms the system and starts the clock.

Open-Meteo forecast
02

Scan every river

Walk the river network and, at each point, an AI compares the live satellite view against the map — flagging channels choked by debris, dumping, or overgrowth.

Esri imagery · OpenStreetMap · vision model
03

Model the flood

Pull the real terrain around each blockage and spread the backed-up water over the actual low ground — to see which blocks and roads go under, not a guess-circle.

Copernicus GLO-90 elevation · bathtub inundation
04

Send the crews

Rank every site by the euros of damage it prevents, then output a work order: where to go, what to clear, and by when.

EU JRC depth-damage curves · dispatch ticket

Data & method

Public data, stated plainly.

Every layer is open and checkable. No black boxes.

OpenStreetMapriver network, buildings, roads, hospitals & stations
Esri World Imagerysatellite view for each inspection point
Open-Meteorain forecast and terrain elevation
Copernicus GLO-90digital terrain for the flood model
EU JRC (Huizinga 2017)depth-damage curves behind every € figure
Vision AIreads satellite vs map to spot blocked channels

Flood depth is a first-order estimate from rainfall and the measured blockage; the spread is a bathtub inundation over real terrain, not a full hydrodynamic solve. The terrain, imagery, assets and damage curves are real — the water physics is a transparent MVP, and we say so.
